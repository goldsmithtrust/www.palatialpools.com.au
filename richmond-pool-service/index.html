Seasonal Pool Tips for Richmond

Melbourne’s climate demands year-round pool attention. Here’s our season-by-season guide to keeping your Richmond pool in perfect condition.

Summer (December–February)

Peak Season Pool Care

  • Test water chemistry at least twice per week during heavy use periods — bather load dramatically affects chlorine demand and pH levels.
  • Run your pump for 8–10 hours daily during peak summer. Variable-speed pumps should be set to higher RPM during daylight hours when UV degrades chlorine fastest.
  • Keep cyanuric acid (stabiliser) between 30–50 ppm to protect chlorine from Melbourne’s intense UV. Too much stabiliser is as problematic as too little.
  • Top up water levels more frequently — Melbourne summers can evaporate 25–50mm per week from an uncovered pool, and even more on scorching north-wind days.
Autumn (March–May)

Preparing for the Cool Months

  • Increase skimming frequency as deciduous trees shed leaves. A single weekend of neglect during peak leaf fall can overwhelm your skimmer baskets and block circulation.
  • Reduce pump run times gradually — 6–8 hours per day is usually sufficient as water temperatures drop below 22°C.
  • Service your pool heater before the cooler months arrive. Gas heaters should have burner assemblies cleaned and ignition systems tested; heat pumps need coil cleaning and refrigerant checks.
  • Consider a quality pool cover to retain heat and reduce debris. A good cover can cut heating costs by 50–70% and virtually eliminate leaf cleanup.
Winter (June–August)

Winter Pool Management

  • Never shut down your pool completely for winter in Melbourne’s climate. Stagnant water breeds algae and can damage surfaces. Reduce pump operation to 4–6 hours daily instead.
  • Lower chlorine levels slightly but maintain a residual of at least 1.0 ppm. Algae grows slowly in cold water but will establish if sanitiser drops to zero.
  • Check your pool at least fortnightly for signs of staining, surface degradation, or water level changes that might indicate a leak.
  • Clear gutters and downpipes near your pool area — Melbourne’s winter rainfall can wash garden debris, fertiliser runoff, and sediment into uncovered pools.
Spring (September–November)

Spring Pool Revival

  • Book a professional spring opening service 4–6 weeks before you plan to start swimming. This allows time to address any issues discovered during the inspection.
  • Shock dose your pool with a quality calcium hypochlorite product to oxidise built-up organic matter and kill any dormant algae spores.
  • Service all equipment: clean cartridge filters or perform a sand filter media change if overdue, inspect pump seals, test chlorinator cell condition, and verify heater operation.
  • Check pool fencing and safety equipment to ensure compliance with Victorian regulations before the swim season begins.
