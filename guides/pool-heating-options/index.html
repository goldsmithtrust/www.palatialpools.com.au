Pool Heating Overview for Melbourne

Melbourne's climate presents a unique challenge for pool heating. With water temperatures dropping to 8–12°C in winter and rarely exceeding 22–24°C in summer without heating, most pool owners who want to swim comfortably for more than 3–4 months of the year need some form of pool heating.

The good news is that Melbourne also offers ideal conditions for the most efficient heating technologies. The city's mild-to-warm summers make heat pumps incredibly efficient, the abundance of sunshine (despite Melbourne's rainy reputation) supports solar heating for much of the year, and reliable natural gas infrastructure makes gas heating a viable option for rapid heating.

The three main pool heating technologies available in Australia are:

  • Heat pumps (air-source): Extract heat from ambient air and transfer it to pool water. Most energy-efficient powered option.
  • Gas heaters: Burn natural gas or LPG to heat water directly. Fastest heating but highest running cost.
  • Solar heating: Use roof-mounted collectors to capture free heat from the sun. Lowest running cost but weather-dependent.

Heat Pumps Explained

A professionally installed heat pump alongside existing pool equipment

A pool heat pump works on the same principle as a reverse-cycle air conditioner — it extracts heat energy from the surrounding air and transfers it to the pool water. Even when the air feels cold, there is still heat energy available to be harvested (heat pumps work effectively down to about 5–8°C ambient temperature).

How It Works

  1. A fan draws ambient air across an evaporator coil containing cold refrigerant gas.
  2. The refrigerant absorbs heat from the air and evaporates into a gas.
  3. A compressor pressurises the gas, dramatically increasing its temperature.
  4. The hot gas passes through a heat exchanger (condenser) where pool water flows on the other side, absorbing the heat.
  5. The cooled refrigerant returns to liquid form and the cycle repeats.

Key Specifications

  • Coefficient of Performance (COP): 4.0–6.0 — this means for every 1 kW of electricity consumed, the heat pump produces 4–6 kW of heat. This is what makes heat pumps so efficient.
  • Heating output: 12–30 kW (residential models)
  • Power consumption: 2–5 kW (electrical input)
  • Heat-up time: 24–72 hours from cold (depending on pool size and ambient temperature)
  • Lifespan: 10–20 years

Full-Inverter vs On/Off Heat Pumps

Modern full-inverter heat pumps (similar to inverter air conditioners) can modulate their compressor speed to match the heating demand. They are significantly quieter, more energy-efficient, and better at maintaining a steady temperature compared to older on/off models. At Palatial Pools, we exclusively install full-inverter heat pumps for new installations.

💡 Melbourne advantage: Heat pumps are particularly well-suited to Melbourne because the city's moderate climate means the heat pump operates at high COP values for most of the swimming season. Even in winter, Melbourne's daytime temperatures of 10–15°C allow modern inverter heat pumps to operate effectively (though at reduced COP). This makes heat pumps the most cost-effective powered heating option for the Melbourne climate.

Gas Heaters Explained

A natural gas pool heater — ideal for rapid on-demand heating

Gas pool heaters burn natural gas (or LPG) to heat water directly. Pool water flows through a combustion chamber where gas burners heat a copper heat exchanger, transferring heat directly to the passing water. Gas heaters are the fastest way to heat a pool and can raise water temperature by approximately 1°C per hour regardless of ambient conditions.

Key Specifications

  • Heating output: 100–400 MJ/hr (residential models)
  • Efficiency: 80–95% (modern condensing models reach 95%+)
  • Heat-up time: 12–24 hours from cold — the fastest option
  • Fuel type: Natural gas or LPG (bottled gas)
  • Lifespan: 8–15 years (depending on usage pattern)

When Gas Is the Best Choice

  • Occasional heating: If you only heat the pool a few times per month (weekends, events), gas is practical because it heats quickly and you only pay for gas when it's running.
  • Spa heating: Spas need to reach 36–38°C quickly — gas is ideal for this rapid heat-up.
  • Winter swimming: Gas heaters work at full power regardless of ambient temperature, making them reliable in Melbourne's coldest months.
  • Quick heat-up: If you decide Friday afternoon that you want the pool warm by Saturday morning, a gas heater is your only option for such rapid response.

Considerations

  • Running cost: Gas is significantly more expensive than heat pump heating — typically 3–5× the cost per degree of heating. This is the main drawback.
  • Gas connection: Requires a natural gas supply (standard in most Melbourne suburbs) with adequate capacity, or an LPG tank. A licensed gasfitter must install the connection.
  • Emissions: Gas heaters produce CO₂ emissions. As Victoria moves towards electrification, some pool owners are choosing heat pumps for environmental reasons.
  • Ventilation: Gas heaters must be installed in well-ventilated areas with adequate clearances as per Australian standards. They cannot be installed in enclosed plant rooms without specific ventilation provisions.

Solar Pool Heating Explained

Solar pool heating uses roof-mounted collectors (typically made from polypropylene or rubber matting) through which pool water is pumped. As the water passes through the collectors, it absorbs heat from the sun before returning to the pool. It is the most environmentally friendly and cheapest-to-run option, but is dependent on weather and daylight hours.

How It Works

  1. The pool pump pushes water up to roof-mounted collector panels (or ground-mounted, if roof space is unavailable).
  2. Water flows through narrow tubes or channels within the collectors, absorbing solar heat.
  3. Heated water returns to the pool, raising the overall temperature gradually over the day.
  4. A controller with temperature sensors automates the process — diverting water to the collectors when they are hotter than the pool water, and bypassing them when they're not.

Key Specifications

  • Collector area required: 80–100% of the pool's surface area (e.g., a 50 m² pool needs 40–50 m² of collector panels)
  • Temperature gain: 4–10°C above ambient pool temperature (weather dependent)
  • Running cost: Essentially free — the pool pump circulates the water through existing plumbing (a small additional head from the collectors increases pump energy slightly)
  • Lifespan: 15–25 years (quality panels)
  • Best months in Melbourne: October–April

Limitations

  • Weather dependent: Cloudy, rainy days produce minimal heating. Melbourne's variable weather means solar heating is unreliable as a sole heating source.
  • No heating at night: The pool loses heat overnight, and solar only replenishes it during the following day. A pool blanket is essential to minimise overnight heat loss.
  • Winter performance: Shorter days, lower sun angle, and frequent cloud cover make solar heating largely ineffective in Melbourne from May to September.
  • Roof space required: A significant area of north-facing roof is needed. Not all properties have suitable roof space, and heritage overlays or body corporate restrictions may prevent installation.
  • Aesthetics: Black collector panels on the roof are visible and may not suit all properties.

Climate Suitability for Melbourne

Melbourne's climate is characterised by warm-to-hot summers, cool-to-cold winters, and highly variable weather within individual days. Here is how each heating type performs in Melbourne's specific conditions:

Heat Pumps in Melbourne

Heat pumps are ideally suited to Melbourne's climate. During the primary swimming season (October–April), daytime temperatures of 18–35°C allow heat pumps to operate at their highest efficiency (COP 5.0–6.0). Even in winter, Melbourne's relatively mild daytime temperatures of 10–15°C keep heat pumps functional, though at reduced efficiency (COP 2.5–3.5). Modern full-inverter models with defrost capability can operate effectively down to 5°C ambient temperature.

Gas Heaters in Melbourne

Gas heaters work equally well in all Melbourne conditions because their performance is not affected by ambient temperature. This makes them the most reliable option for year-round heating, particularly for pool owners who want to swim through winter. Melbourne's extensive natural gas network means most homes have a gas connection available. However, with Victoria's gas substitution roadmap and rising gas prices, some pool owners are transitioning from gas to heat pumps.

Solar in Melbourne

Despite Melbourne's reputation for unpredictable weather, the city receives approximately 2,200 hours of sunshine per year — enough for effective solar pool heating from October through April. Solar can extend the swimming season by 2–4 months compared to an unheated pool. However, solar alone is insufficient for year-round heating in Melbourne. The optimal strategy for many Melbourne pool owners is solar combined with a heat pump or gas heater for shoulder months and winter.

Installation Requirements

Heat Pump Installation

  • Location: Outdoors with good airflow — the unit needs unrestricted air circulation to operate efficiently. Minimum 1 metre clearance on all sides.
  • Electrical: Dedicated circuit, typically 15A or 20A single-phase (smaller models) or 32A three-phase (larger models). Must be installed by a licensed electrician. An RCD (safety switch) is required.
  • Plumbing: Connected to the pool's return plumbing, typically after the filter and chlorinator. 40mm or 50mm pipe connections.
  • Noise considerations: Position away from bedrooms and neighbours. Modern inverter models produce 38–50 dB — comparable to a quiet conversation — but placement still matters, especially for overnight operation.
  • Base: Level concrete pad or heavy-duty rubber anti-vibration mounts.

Gas Heater Installation

  • Gas supply: Natural gas connection with adequate capacity. A licensed gasfitter assesses whether the existing meter and line can support the heater's gas demand. Upgrading the gas meter or running a larger supply line may be necessary.
  • Ventilation: Must be installed in a well-ventilated area with clearances as per the manufacturer's specifications and Australian Standard AS 5601 (Gas Installations). Cannot be enclosed in a sealed room.
  • Flue/exhaust: The exhaust must be directed away from windows, doors, and areas where people gather. Some models have powered flues that allow more flexible placement.
  • Electrical: Low-voltage connection for the ignition and controller — typically plugs into a standard power point, but should be on a weatherproofed outlet near the equipment pad.

Solar Installation

  • Roof assessment: North-facing roof is ideal. West-facing is acceptable but slightly less effective. The roof must be structurally sound to support the weight of collectors and water. A professional structural assessment may be required for older roofs.
  • Council and heritage: Some Melbourne councils and heritage overlays have restrictions on roof installations. Check with your local council before proceeding.
  • Plumbing: Pipes run from the equipment pad up to the roof collectors and back. This involves roof penetrations that must be properly weatherproofed.
  • Controller: An automated controller with temperature sensors is installed to manage water flow to the collectors based on available solar heat.

Pool Blankets & Heat Retention

Regardless of which heating system you choose, a pool blanket (also called a thermal cover or solar blanket) is the single most impactful addition you can make to reduce heating costs. Here's why:

  • Up to 70% of pool heat is lost through surface evaporation. A blanket reduces this loss by 50–70%.
  • In Melbourne's cool evenings, an uncovered heated pool can lose 3–5°C overnight. With a blanket, this drops to 0.5–1.5°C.
  • A blanket reduces chemical consumption (less evaporation = less chemical loss) and keeps debris out of the pool.
  • For solar heating, a blanket is essential — without it, you lose most of the heat gained during the day each night.

Pool blankets range from $200 for a basic bubble cover to $2,000+ for an automated roller system with a high-quality 500-micron cover. The payback period through heating cost savings is typically 1–2 seasons.

Our recommendation for Melbourne: For most Melbourne pool owners who swim regularly from October to April, we recommend a full-inverter heat pump combined with a quality pool blanket. This combination provides the lowest total cost of ownership, reliable heating regardless of weather, and the ability to extend the swimming season into autumn and even winter if desired. For pool owners with suitable roof space and a focus on minimal running costs, adding solar heating as a supplement further reduces costs during the warmer months.
