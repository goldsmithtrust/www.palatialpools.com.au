Why Is My Pool Green? How to Fix It Fast

A green pool is an algae problem — but the fix is straightforward if you act quickly.

⏱ 5 min read
Home › Blog › Fix Green Pool Fast
📅 10 April 2026 ✍️ Kieran Goldsmith, Palatial Pools 🏷️ Water Chemistry · Algae · Maintenance ⏱ 5 min read

Nothing ruins a Melbourne summer like a green pool. You've been looking forward to a swim, you lift the cover, and you're greeted by something that looks more like a swamp than a swimming pool. Don't panic — this is one of the most common pool problems we see, and it's fixable.

Why Does a Pool Turn Green?

A green pool is almost always caused by algae growth. Algae thrives when:

  • Chlorine levels drop too low (below 1 ppm)
  • The pH is out of balance (above 7.8 or below 7.2)
  • The filter isn't running long enough
  • The pool hasn't been brushed or vacuumed regularly
  • There's been heavy rain, which dilutes chemicals and introduces phosphates

Melbourne's hot summers create perfect conditions for algae — warm water, lots of sunlight, and heavy use. A pool can go from clear to green in as little as 48 hours if the chemistry drops off.

The Palatial Pools Green Pool Recovery Process

When we attend a green pool recovery, here's the process we follow:

Step 1: Test the Water

Before adding any chemicals, we test the water to understand exactly what we're dealing with. pH, chlorine, alkalinity, stabiliser, and phosphate levels all affect how we treat the pool.

Step 2: Balance the pH

Chlorine is far less effective outside the pH range of 7.2–7.6. We adjust pH first using pH Down (sodium bisulphate) or pH Up (sodium carbonate) before adding any sanitiser.

Step 3: Shock the Pool

We add a high dose of liquid chlorine — typically 5–10 times the normal maintenance dose — to kill the algae. For a heavily green pool, we may use calcium hypochlorite granules for a more powerful shock.

Step 4: Add Algaecide

A quality algaecide prevents regrowth and helps break down the dead algae. We use Pool Pro-approved algaecides that are compatible with all pool types including fibreglass and vinyl.

Step 5: Run the Filter 24/7

The filter needs to run continuously until the water clears — typically 24–72 hours depending on severity. We backwash the filter every 12 hours to prevent it from clogging with dead algae.

Step 6: Vacuum to Waste

Once the algae is dead (the water will turn cloudy grey/white), we vacuum the pool to waste — bypassing the filter entirely — to remove the dead algae from the pool without recirculating it.

Important: Never swim in a green pool. Algae can harbour harmful bacteria including E. coli. Wait until the water is clear and chlorine levels are back to 1–3 ppm before swimming.

How Long Does It Take to Fix a Green Pool?

A mildly green pool (light green, can see the bottom) typically clears within 24–48 hours. A severely green pool (dark green, cannot see the bottom) may take 3–5 days of continuous treatment and filtration.

Preventing It From Happening Again

The best cure is prevention. Here's what we recommend:

  • Regular professional servicing — fortnightly in summer, monthly in winter. Palatial Pools' regular maintenance service starts from $95 + chemicals.
  • Run your pump long enough — 8 hours/day in summer minimum. An inverter pump like the Neptune X-Eco makes this affordable.
  • Keep a chlorine float — a slow-release chlorine float provides continuous low-level sanitisation between services.
  • Brush weekly — algae starts on pool surfaces before it takes over the water. Regular brushing removes the spores before they can establish.
  • Consider a Naked Pools freshwater system — mineral-based water treatment is more stable and less prone to algae than traditional chlorine systems.
