Signs Your Pool Has a Leak

Don't let a slow leak become a $10,000 repair. Here's how to spot a pool leak early.

⏱ 5 min read
Home › Blog › Signs Your Pool Has a Leak
📅 14 April 2026 ✍️ Kieran Goldsmith, Palatial Pools 🏷️ Leak Detection · Maintenance ⏱ 5 min read

A pool leak is one of the most common — and most costly — problems Melbourne pool owners face. Left undetected, even a small leak can cause significant structural damage, undermine your landscaping, and add hundreds of dollars to your water bill every month.

The good news: if you catch it early, most leaks are straightforward to fix. Here's how to tell if your pool is leaking, and what Palatial Pools can do about it.

The Bucket Test: Your First Step

Before calling a professional, do the bucket test. Fill a bucket with pool water to about 2cm from the top and place it on the first pool step. Mark the water level inside the bucket and on the pool wall. After 24 hours, compare the two levels.

  • If both levels dropped equally — you're losing water to evaporation, which is normal.
  • If the pool level dropped more than the bucket — you likely have a leak.

Melbourne pools typically lose 2–3mm per day to evaporation in summer. Anything beyond 5mm/day warrants investigation.

7 Signs Your Pool Is Leaking

  1. Water level dropping more than 5mm per day — especially if it's consistent regardless of weather.
  2. Cracks in the pool shell, coping, or tiles — even hairline cracks can allow significant water loss.
  3. Wet or soggy patches in your yard — particularly near the pool equipment or return jets.
  4. Unusually high water bills — if you're topping up the pool frequently, the water has to go somewhere.
  5. Air in the pump or filter — bubbles in the return jets can indicate a suction-side leak.
  6. Equipment running dry or losing prime — the pump struggles to maintain pressure when there's a leak on the suction side.
  7. Loose or lifting tiles — water infiltration behind tiles causes them to lift and crack.

Important: Don't confuse a leak with splash loss or backwash discharge. A genuine structural leak will be consistent and measurable with the bucket test.

Common Leak Locations in Melbourne Pools

In our experience servicing Melbourne pools, the most common leak locations are:

  • Skimmer box — the seal between the skimmer and the pool shell is a common failure point, especially in older pools.
  • Return jets and fittings — the rubber gaskets degrade over time and allow water to escape around the fitting.
  • Pool light conduit — water can travel along the conduit pipe if the seal fails.
  • Underground plumbing — tree roots, ground movement, and corrosion can crack buried pipes.
  • Pool shell cracks — particularly in concrete pools that have experienced ground movement.

Don't Wait — Leaks Get Worse

A leak that loses 5mm per day is losing approximately 250 litres per day from a standard Melbourne pool. That's 7,500 litres per month — and the structural damage from prolonged water loss can cost far more to repair than the leak itself.
